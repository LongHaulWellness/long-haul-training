# Coaching principles

> How you train. The coach follows these rules when planning, so write down what you actually believe in and what works for you. If you're not sure yet, talk it through with the coach and fill it in together. Delete the hint lines as you go.

## 0. Philosophy
- The approach, coach, book or school of thought you want to follow, if any, and what you take from it.
- What matters most to you beyond performance (enjoyment, consistency, health, community).
- How much the plan should bend to your feel on the day.

## 1. Intensity system
- How intensity is prescribed: pace, heart rate, power, RPE, or a mix (e.g. pace on the road, HR and effort off-road).
- Where the current zones/paces live (usually `athlete_profile.md`).
- Easy/hard distribution (e.g. ~80% easy / 20% quality) and how it changes in recovery or base phases.
- Which cross-training counts toward aerobic load, and how.

Other rules:
- Number of sessions per sport per week.
- Where strength and mobility go, and how they sit relative to hard sessions.
- What to do when a day falls through (swap rules, minimum doses).
- How races and group sessions replace planned sessions.

## 3. Guardrails
- Health-specific rules (injury history, illness, nutrition, anything your professionals asked for).
- When a day gets downgraded (e.g. bad recovery → easy only) and when to stop and see a doctor.
- Fueling and hydration rules.

## 4. Progression
- How volume and intensity grow (e.g. one variable at a time, +10–15% per week).
- Deload rhythm (e.g. every 4th week at −30%).
- Target volume ranges for this season.

## 5. Feedback loop
- Where session notes go (e.g. Strava descriptions) and that the coach reads them.
- What the weekly review covers (see the checklist in `README.md`).
- How disagreements are handled: the athlete's experience overrides the plan, and the reason is noted in the week file.
